Security and trust

What protects your account, and what does not

This site asks members to create an account and, for some tournaments, to pay an entry fee. This page sets out exactly what protects that and, just as importantly, what does not yet.

Everything in the first five sections is implemented and running. Nothing there is planned or aspirational. Anything in that category is in Not yet done at the bottom, named plainly.

Payments

Payment confirmations are cryptographically verified

  • Every status update from Paynow is checked against a SHA-512 signature derived from our integration key A confirmation that is unsigned, altered in transit, or sent by anyone other than Paynow is rejected outright and logged with its source address.
  • A rejected confirmation cannot change a payment status or confirm a tournament place Verification happens before the payment record is even looked up.

A payment can only be acted on by its owner

  • Checking a payment progress requires being signed in as the member who made it
  • Test-mode payment tools are refused entirely outside local development Three independent guards enforce this: a configuration default, a runtime check, and an ownership check. The application refuses to start at all if test payments are switched on in production.

Your account

Optional two-factor authentication

  • Time-based one-time codes from any authenticator app
  • Locked for 60 seconds after five wrong codes This is what stops an attacker simply trying every six-digit combination.

Passwords are never stored

  • Only a salted PBKDF2 hash is kept, so your actual password is not recoverable by anyone, including administrators
  • New passwords are checked against common-password lists, minimum length, and similarity to your own details

Connection and session

  • HTTPS on every pageTraffic between your browser and this site is encrypted in transit.
  • Session and CSRF cookies are marked Secure in productionThey are never transmitted over an unencrypted connection.
  • CSRF protection on every state-changing requestAnother site cannot cause your browser to submit an action as you.
  • The site only answers to its own domainHost headers are restricted, which is what prevents forged password-reset links pointing somewhere else.

Configuration that fails safe

The most dangerous failures are the quiet ones: a system that looks healthy while doing nothing. This one is built to refuse to start rather than run in a state that only appears to work.

  • Refuses to start in production with a development signing key
  • Refuses to start with test-mode payments enabled
  • Refuses to start configured to send email by a method that cannot work on our host
  • Member and tournament data is scoped to its club and held in a managed PostgreSQL database with persistent storage

Not yet done

A security page that lists only strengths is not worth reading. These are real and current.

  • Email notifications are not yet liveAnnouncements, tournament alerts and password resets are written but not yet delivering. Until that is switched on, treat the alerts page inside the site as the reliable channel.
  • HSTS is not enabledHTTPS is enforced by our host, but browsers are not yet instructed to refuse an unencrypted connection outright.
  • No independent security auditEverything above is our own work and our own testing. It has not been reviewed by an outside party.
  • Two-factor authentication is optional, not requiredStrongly recommended for administrator and organiser accounts.

Found a problem?

If you believe you have found a security issue, please report it to your club administrator rather than posting it publicly. Reports are welcome and will not be held against the person making them.

This page describes the system as deployed. If you find a claim here that does not match what the site actually does, that is a bug and we want to hear about it.
